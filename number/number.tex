\textbf{Linear diophantine equations}: Let $a,b,c \in \mathbb{N}$. 
The equation $ax+by=c$ has integer solutions iff $c$ is a multiple of $gcd(a,b)$.
If $(x_0,y_0)$ is a solution for the equation (c.f. Extended Euclidean Algorithm), 
then all integer solutions are given by $(x_0-b't, y_0+a't)$ with $t \in \mathbb{N}, 
a'= \frac{a}{gcd(a,b)}, b'=\frac{b}{gcd(a,b)}$.\\
\textbf{Chinese reminder theorem:} Let $n_1,\ldots,n_k \in \mathbb{N}$ pairwise coprime,
and $b1,\ldots,bk \in \mathbb{N}$ The system of linear congruencies $x \equiv b_i \mod (n_i)$
for $1\leq i \leq k$ has a unique solution $\mod \prod_{i=1}^k n_i$, constructed as follows:
Let $a_i = \frac{N}{n_i}$ and $c_i$ its inverse$\mod n_i$. Then, $x\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{k} a_i b_i c_i \mod \prod_{i=1}^k n_i$
